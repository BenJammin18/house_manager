# house_manager

A proactive, permission-gated household management agent for a two-person family. It isn't a to-do list you have to remember to check — it captures things in plain language, tracks them across every domain of running a household, nudges you before you forget, and acts on your behalf where it safely can, asking permission by text everywhere it can't.

Built for a household with ADHD/"type B" dynamics in mind: the point is to move mental load *off* both people, not to add another app to check.

## What it does today

All 8 phases of the original build plan, plus a Phase 9 asset/vendor registry, are implemented and tested (41 unit tests + 13 live-API tests against real Anthropic/Twilio/Plaid/Google services).

### Capture & tracking
- **Natural-language capture** — type or text a sentence ("vet appt for Fluffy next Tuesday 3pm," "pay the water bill by the 15th, it's $62") and Claude extracts a structured item: domain, title, due date, assignee, priority, domain-specific details (amount/payee for bills, vet/pet name for pet care, etc.). Asks for clarification instead of guessing when the input is genuinely ambiguous.
- **Unified item model** spanning every domain: chores, home maintenance, pets, baby care, bills, finance, social/family scheduling, and prescriptions.
- **Dashboard** — open items grouped by domain, manual add/complete/skip, all backed by the same natural-language capture pipeline.

### Calendar
- One Google OAuth link (per household member) covers both Calendar and Gmail.
- Any item with a specific due time auto-syncs to the linked Google Calendar — no approval needed (Tier A).
- **Multi-calendar conflict detection** — reads every linked calendar (each spouse's personal calendar + shared family calendar) and flags overlapping events across different calendars.
- **Trip-coverage suggestions** — detects trip-tagged social items and, if the household has open pet/baby-care items, proactively creates a "arrange coverage" item a week ahead of the trip.

### Nudging & escalation (SMS via Twilio)
- Deterministic escalation: a soft reminder when something's due soon or just overdue, a firmer nudge after 24 hours still open.
- **LLM-judged escalation** — after ~72 hours still open, Claude decides whether to re-nudge, reassign to the other household member, or mark it urgent, and drafts the message.
- **Daily digest** — a prioritized, domain-grouped summary of what's open, generated by Claude and texted once a day.

### Action framework — the "agent" part
Actions are tiered by how much autonomy makes sense:
- **Tier A (automatic, no approval)** — calendar sync, trip-coverage suggestions, bill/budget reconciliation against real bank data.
- **Tier B (auto-approve rules, otherwise asks)** — e.g. bill payment confirmation. You can set rules like "auto-confirm bills under $50 from City Water" that skip the text-and-wait step; anything else texts a yes/no confirmation first.
- **Tier C (always asks, never auto-approved)** — supply ordering and appointment booking, where no real ordering/booking API exists. These are honestly scoped as "confirm you did it yourself," not "the agent did it for you" — there's no bank-payment or Amazon/booking integration, so "executing" one of these means marking the item done once you reply YES, not moving money or placing a real order.
- **Two-way SMS approvals** — a proposal texts out, you reply YES/NO, a signature-verified webhook resolves it.

### Finance (Plaid)
- Link real bank accounts (read-only — no ability to move money).
- Transactions sync hourly.
- **Bill reconciliation** — auto-marks a bill item done when a matching real transaction shows up (payee + amount match).
- **Budget-pace alerts** — flags a category as an item when month-to-date spend materially outpaces the prorated monthly budget.
- **LLM-proposed starting budgets** — Claude groups your real spending into sensible categories; all dollar math is computed in code, not by the model (see note below).

### Email (Gmail)
- Spam gets moved to Trash (never permanently deleted).
- Important mail gets labeled (`house_manager/important`) and surfaced in the digest.
- Emails describing a real event (reservation confirmations, appointment notices) get turned into items that flow through the same calendar-sync pipeline as manually captured items.
- Only processes new mail going forward from when an account is linked — no mailbox backfill.

### Assets & vendors
- Register household assets (AC units, dishwasher, oven, water heater, etc.) with a category, location, and an optional linked vendor.
- Register service vendors (HVAC, lawn care, pest control, plumbing, etc.) with a name, phone, and service type.
- One click — "Something broke" on an asset, or "Need service" on a vendor — creates a maintenance item and immediately texts a booking-confirmation proposal pre-filled with the vendor's name and phone, reusing the same Tier C book-appointment flow as everything else.
- **Natural-language capture recognizes registered assets** — typing "the AC is blowing warm air" matches it to "Upstairs AC unit" and automatically pulls in that asset's linked vendor.
- These quick-actions don't assign a specific person — either spouse can act on the text, and it's texted to both.

## Example workflows

**"I forgot to pay the water bill again."**
Text or type "pay water bill by the 15th, $62.40" → it becomes a bill item. As the due date approaches you get a text reminder; if it's still open 24 hours after due, a firmer one. Tap "text to confirm paid" (or it's proposed automatically once a matching Tier B setup exists) and reply YES once you've paid it — it marks itself done. If your bank account is linked, this can happen without you doing anything: the moment a matching transaction shows up, the bill auto-completes.

**"We're going on a trip, did we sort out the dog?"**
Capture "trip to Denver next month" as a social item. If you have an open pet-care item, house_manager automatically creates a "arrange pet coverage" item a week before the trip, so it's on your radar instead of a 6am scramble.

**"Something's due and nobody's dealing with it."**
An item sits open past its due date, gets nudged, gets nudged again 24 hours later — and if it's still open after that, Claude decides whether to hand it to the other spouse, mark it urgent, or just nudge again, and drafts a message that isn't robotic.

**"Did an email need my attention?"**
A promotional email gets trashed automatically. A pediatrician appointment confirmation email gets turned into a calendar item without you touching it. A note from your kid's school gets labeled important and shows up in your daily digest instead of getting buried.

**"Are we double-booked?"**
You and your spouse each have your calendar linked. If you both end up with overlapping events, house_manager flags it as an item — before it becomes an awkward Tuesday.

**"The AC just died, who do I even call?"**
You'd registered "Upstairs AC unit" with "Joe's HVAC" as its vendor once, ahead of time. Now either type "the AC is blowing warm air" or hit "Something broke" next to the asset on the dashboard — a maintenance item gets created and a text goes out to both of you: "Call to book 'Upstairs AC unit' with Joe's HVAC (555-111-2222)? Reply YES once you've called and booked it." No digging through old emails for the vendor's number.

## Setup

```bash
python3.12+ -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install -e ".[dev]"
cp .env.example .env   # fill in the values below
.venv/bin/alembic upgrade head
PYTHONPATH=. .venv/bin/python scripts/seed_dev_data.py
.venv/bin/uvicorn app.main:app --reload
```

### Required accounts/credentials (see `.env.example` for exact variable names)

| Service | Needed for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Anthropic | Natural-language capture, digest, escalation judgment, budget proposals, email classification | `HOUSE_MANAGER_ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` |
| Twilio | Outbound nudges/digest, two-way SMS approvals | Account SID + (Auth Token or API Key pair) + a phone number. Auth Token specifically is required for inbound webhook signature verification. |
| Google Cloud (OAuth client) | Calendar sync + Gmail triage | One OAuth client, one consent flow covers both — needs the Calendar API and Gmail API enabled, and `.../auth/calendar` + `.../auth/gmail.modify` scopes on the consent screen. Keep the app in "Testing" mode with both household members added as test users to skip Google's verification review. |
| Plaid | Bank-account linking, transaction sync, budget tracking | Sandbox is fine for testing; Development/Production for real accounts. |
| `HOUSE_MANAGER_ENCRYPTION_KEY` | Encrypting stored OAuth/API tokens at rest | Generate with `python -c "from cryptography.fernet import Fernet; print(Fernet.generate_key().decode())"` |

## Architecture

- **Stack:** FastAPI + SQLite + SQLModel/Alembic + APScheduler, server-rendered dashboard (Jinja2 + HTMX), Anthropic/Twilio/Google/Plaid SDKs.
- **Core model:** a single `item` table spans every domain (`chore`, `maintenance`, `pet`, `baby`, `bill`, `finance`, `social`, `prescription`) rather than separate bespoke tables per area.
- **Scheduler jobs** (APScheduler, in-process): `evaluate_due_items` (15 min — escalation + Tier A actions), `sync_transactions_job` (hourly — Plaid), `scan_inbox_job` (30 min — Gmail), `detect_calendar_conflicts_job` (hourly), `send_daily_digest` (daily, 7am).
- Full architecture/design history lives in the project's planning notes; ask for a walkthrough of any specific piece if useful.

## Current limitations

- **Not deployed anywhere public** — runs locally only. Twilio's inbound SMS webhook is fully built and signature-verified but can't receive real texts until the app is hosted somewhere with a public URL.
- **Google OAuth consent hasn't been completed yet** — the auth URL is verified correct, but no calendar/email account is actually linked until someone clicks through it in a browser.
- **No real payment or ordering execution** — bill "payment," supply ordering, and appointment booking are all draft-and-confirm or self-report loops. Nothing moves money or places a real order; there's no bank-payment or e-commerce integration, by design, until a specific one is chosen.
- **No recurrence rollforward** — a recurring item (e.g. weekly trash day) doesn't automatically regenerate itself after being marked done.
- **No authentication on the dashboard** — anyone on the local network can see or change anything. Fine for local household use; relevant if this is ever exposed beyond localhost.
